# Viscous Arm

The Viscous Arm is a 6+1 DOF follower from Viscous Robotics: six revolute joints plus a permanent
gripper, **all seven RS00** RobStride actuators on one classic CAN bus. LeRobot talks to them
directly through `python-can` — no ROS, and no vendor SDK at runtime.

It shares its topology, joint names, and driver stack with the [Maker Arm](./maker.mdx). The
difference is the motors: Maker carries RS02 at `shoulder_lift` and `elbow_flex` for its land
duty cycle; the Viscous Arm is all-RS00 because it runs neutrally buoyant underwater, where the
gravity load those two joints fight on a bench largely disappears.

## Bring-up

Everything below is a **one-time procedure for a newly assembled arm**. Skip to
[Usage](#usage) on an arm that has already been through it.

<Tip warning={true}>

`private` and `MIT` are persistent, mutually exclusive motor protocols. The
[Maker Arm SDK](https://github.com/makermods-robotics/maker-arm-sdk) speaks only private; LeRobot
speaks only MIT. Switching to MIT costs you ID assignment, zeroing, and every capture tool, and
LeRobot has no replacement for any of them.

**Do all SDK-side steps (1–5) first. The protocol switch is the last hardware step.**

</Tip>

### Joint map

Joint number, CAN id, and LeRobot joint name are one-to-one. There is no translation table.

| J   | Function       | CAN id | LeRobot name    | Viscous  | Maker |
| --- | -------------- | -----: | --------------- | -------- | ----- |
| J1  | base yaw       |      1 | `shoulder_pan`  | RS00     | RS00  |
| J2  | shoulder pitch |      2 | `shoulder_lift` | **RS00** | RS02  |
| J3  | elbow pitch    |      3 | `elbow_flex`    | **RS00** | RS02  |
| J4  | wrist pitch    |      4 | `wrist_flex`    | RS00     | RS00  |
| J5  | wrist yaw      |      5 | `wrist_yaw`     | RS00     | RS00  |
| J6  | wrist roll     |      6 | `wrist_roll`    | RS00     | RS00  |
| J7  | gripper        |      7 | `gripper`       | RS00     | RS00  |

In `RobstrideMotorsBus` tables RS00 is model `O0`.

### Prerequisites

A **CANable 2.0 Pro** on slcan firmware. It is a native USB CDC-ACM device, needs no driver, and
appears as `/dev/cu.usbmodem*` on macOS, `/dev/ttyACM*` on Linux. The RobStride CH340/CH341
debug dongle is mute on CAN from Apple Silicon macOS even with WCH's driver installed — do not
spend time on it.

Install the SDK, used for steps 1–5 only:

```bash
git clone https://github.com/makermods-robotics/maker-arm-sdk.git
cd maker-arm-sdk
uv venv --python 3.12 .venv
uv pip install --python .venv/bin/python -e .
export CANPORT=/dev/cu.usbmodem1101   # your adapter; `ls /dev/cu.*`
```

<Tip warning={true}>

The SDK defaults to `--backend socketcan --channel can0`, which is Linux-only. On macOS pass
`--backend slcan --port "$CANPORT"` to **every** SDK command shown below.

</Tip>

### 1. Assign CAN ids 1–7

Every motor ships as id `127`. Protocol Type 7 addresses a motor _by its current id_, so two
motors still at 127 on one bus get reassigned together.

The constraint is precisely: **only one motor still at 127 may be on the bus at a time.** Motors
you have already assigned can stay connected — their ids no longer collide. So you never unplug
anything; you build the chain up one motor at a time.

This is not a firmware flash. It is a config write that takes effect immediately, with a
save-to-flash sent after as a fallback, and it is repeatable.

For each joint in order J1 → J7:

```bash
# 1. connect the next motor, then confirm the bus is what you expect:
#    every id already assigned, plus exactly one motor at 127
./.venv/bin/maker-arm scan --backend slcan --port "$CANPORT" --max-id 127

# 2. assign it (N = the joint number)
./.venv/bin/maker-arm assign-id --backend slcan --port "$CANPORT" \
    --current-id 127 --new-id N

# 3. verify the chain grew by one
./.venv/bin/maker-arm scan --backend slcan --port "$CANPORT" --max-id 10
```

`assign-id` refuses if the current id does not answer, and refuses if the new id is already in
use. Treat those as a backstop, not a substitute for the scan in step 1.

Scanning between every motor also verifies the daisy chain incrementally: a bad connector shows
up on the joint that caused it, rather than after all seven are attached.

Raw encoder positions at this stage are arbitrary — a joint reading 4.5 rad is normal. Zeroing is
step 2.

### 2. Set the mechanical zero

**Define and photograph the reference pose before this step.** Every limit captured in step 3 and
every leader factor in step 4 is expressed against it. If it cannot be reproduced after a
teardown, steps 3 and 4 must be re-run. Use a repeatable folded resting pose with the gripper
fully open.

With the arm torque-free and the joint held at the reference pose:

```bash
./.venv/bin/maker-arm zero --motor N --backend slcan --port "$CANPORT"
```

The command requires an explicit target so an operator cannot zero the whole arm by accident.

Zero **here, in the private protocol**. MIT-mode `set_zero` persistence across a power cycle is
unverified; if a calibration ever vanishes after a power cycle, this is why.

Verify with `maker-arm scan` — every joint should read near 0 rad at the reference pose.

### 3. Capture this arm's joint limits

The limits in `MakerFollowerConfig` are Maker arm #02's mechanical stops. They do not describe a
Viscous Arm. Capture your own against a profile of your own — never point `--config` at a
production profile:

```bash
./.venv/bin/python tools/engineering/calibration/capture_maker_limits.py \
    --config /path/to/viscous_arm_v1.yaml \
    --backend slcan --port "$CANPORT" \
    --json /path/to/rom-raw.json
```

It connects **without enabling** — no torque, safe to move by hand — and polls at 10 Hz while you
push each joint to both mechanical stops. Enter writes the backed-off limits into the YAML (with
a `.bak`) plus a raw JSON artifact; Ctrl-C exits writing nothing. It needs an interactive
terminal: without a TTY it monitors but can never write.

Keep the JSON. It is the provenance for every number that reaches the follower config.

<Tip warning={true}>

**Verify every joint actually reached both stops.** The tool only skips a joint whose travel is
under 0.3 rad, which is far too permissive to catch a partial sweep — a joint swept through 20% of
its range passes silently and writes a range that fences off most of its workspace.

The cheap check is to compare travel per joint against a known-good arm of the same design. On the
first Viscous capture, six joints landed within a few percent of the Maker Arm and `wrist_yaw`
came in at 36° against Maker's 176° — a partial sweep, not a mechanical limit. Re-running with
`--motor-id 5` rewrote that one joint and left the six good captures untouched, and it then agreed
with Maker to 0.1%.

A second tell: if a joint's captured max is below a position you have already seen it resting at,
it never reached a stop.

</Tip>

### 4. Capture the Star leader ranges and mapping

```bash
./.venv/bin/python tools/engineering/calibration/capture_star_ranges.py \
    --star-port /dev/cu.wchusbserial10 \
    --out /path/to/star-ranges.json
```

Leave **both arms torque-free** — this reads the leader only and drives nothing, so a powered
follower is just a hazard. Sweep every Star joint to both stops while it polls, then Enter writes
the per-servo min/max/travel JSON; Ctrl-C discards.

<Tip warning={true}>

**Sanity-check the travel per servo before you trust the capture.** A joint you did not push all
the way reports a short range and silently shrinks that joint's factor. Servos 0–6 on a
known-good Star Arm 102 (Maker-era capture) read roughly 217, 200, 272, 197, 133, 320 and 59°.
Anything well under its counterpart here is a partial sweep, not a mechanical limit — re-run the
capture.

</Tip>

The mapping is then arithmetic, not another capture. For each joint:

```
factor = sign × (follower travel / Star travel)
```

Follower travel is `hi − lo` from the limits you captured in step 3; Star travel is `travel_deg`
for the servo that drives that joint. The signs come from the Maker preset
(`config_rebot_102_leader_maker.py`) — `+, −, −, −, −, +, −` in joint order — and carry over
unless your arm is built mirrored. Write the seven results into the preset's `joint_directions` in
`config_rebot_102_leader_viscous.py`.

The leader preset **only scales and flips — it adds no offset.** The leader's zero must coincide
with the follower's zero, so calibrate the Star folded to match _this_ arm's reference pose.

### 5. Persist the motor-side CAN watchdog

```bash
./.venv/bin/python tools/experimental/lerobot/persist_can_timeout.py --ids 1,2,3,4,5,6,7
```

Expect all seven to report "written and saved to flash". If the watchdog is too short, motors
silently drop torque partway through teleop, which reads exactly like a gain or mapping fault.
Fallback: switch back to private and re-persist with `--timeout-ms` raised to 1000. Setting it to
`0` disables the motor-side watchdog entirely — last resort, log it, restore it as soon as
possible.

### 6. Switch to the MIT protocol

Point of no return for the SDK tooling. Do not start until steps 1–5 are done and their artifacts
are saved.

```bash
./.venv/bin/python tools/experimental/lerobot/switch_protocol.py --ids 1,2,3,4,5,6,7 --to mit
# power-cycle the motors, then confirm the private protocol is dead:
./.venv/bin/maker-arm scan --backend slcan --port "$CANPORT" --max-id 8   # expect: none found
```

Underneath this is private-protocol Type 25: magic bytes `1,2,3,4,5,6` then `2` for MIT (`0` for
private); each motor acks on `(motor_id << 8) | 0xFE`. The motor zero from step 2 carries over.

Rollback is `--to private` plus a power cycle. Do the round trip once deliberately, so you know
the escape hatch works before you need it.

### 7. Install and verify

```bash
uv sync --extra maker --extra rebot
```

`maker` pulls RobStride/CAN; `rebot` pulls `motorbridge-smart-servo`, the FashionStar UART bus
the Star leader speaks.

Handshake and read with **no torque**, then push a joint by hand and re-read — the value must
change.

<Tip warning={true}>

**Verify the `O0` model mapping once.** The upstream O-series and RS-series numbering are
suspected to be offset (`O2` is ±20 Nm/±33 rad/s, a different tier). On an all-RS00 arm this
assumption covers _every_ joint, so if it is wrong, all position and velocity scaling is wrong.
Spin a joint at fixed speed with `tools/experimental/lerobot/mit_spin.py` and compare MIT
`Present_Velocity` against the private-protocol reading; a 44/33x discrepancy means the wrong
model was picked.

</Tip>

## Calibration

Once the arm is in MIT, `lerobot-calibrate` records each motor id and its soft limits against the
zero set in bring-up step 2:

```bash
lerobot-calibrate \
    --robot.type=viscous_follower \
    --robot.port=/dev/cu.usbmodem1101 \
    --robot.id=my_viscous_follower

lerobot-calibrate \
    --teleop.type=rebot_102_leader_viscous \
    --teleop.port=/dev/cu.usbserial-0001 \
    --teleop.id=star_viscous
```

Use a distinct `id` per physical arm and leader so their calibration files do not collide.

## Usage

Same entry points as the Maker Arm — `lerobot-teleoperate`, `lerobot-record`, `lerobot-replay`,
`lerobot-rollout` — with `--robot.type=viscous_follower` and
`--teleop.type=rebot_102_leader_viscous`. See [Maker Arm](./maker.mdx) for the full command forms;
only the type names differ.

Torque-off is the documented safe state and the arm has no brakes, so a torque-off arm settles
under gravity. Support it before disconnecting.

## Tuning gains

<Tip warning={true}>

**Do not copy the Maker Arm's `shoulder_lift` / `elbow_flex` gains.** Maker runs kp 150 and 90
there because those slots carry RS02 motors. On the Viscous Arm they are RS00, holding a
comparable mass against gravity — and on a dry bench with none of the buoyancy the arm is
designed around, so J2/J3 work harder there than they ever will in water. Start well below the
Maker values and walk up, and keep early bench sessions short: an RS00 holding a pose it cannot
comfortably hold sits at high current and heats.

</Tip>

Tuning order: soft tracking → `kp` +10; jitter → `kd` +0.3. Sluggishness at the start of a session
is usually `startup_sync_speed_deg` and resolves itself once the follower has caught up.

Finish with a **60-second hold test**: keep the leader still with the follower under torque. A
joint that releases torque or drops out is the step-5 watchdog misbehaving in MIT mode, not a gain
problem.

## Troubleshooting

**Gripper travel saturates.** Looks exactly like a wrong direction or a bad gain. Check the
configured `joint_limits` first — placeholder limits saturate long before the mechanism does.

**A joint reads a whole turn off after a power cycle.** RobStride multi-turn counts reset. The
follower detects this and applies a ±360° correction when a reading only lands inside the soft
limits after the shift. A reading no single-turn shift explains is treated as a stale zero, and
the arm refuses to move until it is recalibrated — that refusal is the safety check working.

**A third of observations look repeated in a recorded dataset.** Do not use `sync_read`. Bursting
seven requests loses replies to the adapter's shallow receive FIFO. The follower reads one motor
at a time for this reason: about 1 ms per joint, 9.6 ms of a 33 ms period at 30 Hz.

**Motors do not respond at all after step 6.** They are in MIT and the SDK cannot see them — that
is expected. Verify from the LeRobot side, not with `maker-arm scan`.
